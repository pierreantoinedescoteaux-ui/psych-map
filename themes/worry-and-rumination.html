Worry and rumination

The mental loop that replays the past and rehearses the future without ever reaching a verdict.

How it runs — and where to break it

Thinking as avoidance

  1. causeUncertainty feels dangerous
  2. it isn't→causeWorrying feels like doing something about it
  3. →consequenceThreat stays switched on; the problem stays unsolved
  4. put the loop on paper→toolThe thought record

The meeting ended six hours ago and you're still in it, re-running what you said. Or it's 2 a.m. and your mind is holding a planning session nobody scheduled, about things that mostly won't happen. Worry points forward, rumination points backward. Both feel like problem-solving, and the resemblance is the trap: the loop produces very few solutions per hour of running.

Read the restShow less

The entries here cover why the loop is so convincing and what loosens it. The availability heuristic explains why vividly imagined disasters feel probable. The spotlight effect cuts the imagined audience down to size. The thought record and cognitive defusion offer two different moves: examining the thought, or stepping back from it. Graded exposure works on the avoidance that worry quietly builds. Acceptance and commitment therapy, the approach behind defusion, frames the goal as changing your relationship to the thoughts rather than winning arguments with them.
