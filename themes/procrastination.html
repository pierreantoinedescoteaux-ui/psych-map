Procrastination

Knowing exactly what needs doing, wanting it done, and watching yourself do anything else instead.

How it runs — and where to break it

The relief loop

  1. causeThe task carries discomfort — boredom, confusion, fear of doing it badly
  2. now outweighs later→causePresent bias
  3. →consequenceAvoiding it buys instant relief
  4. →consequenceRelief trains the loop to run again
  5. pre-decide the first move→toolImplementation intentions

The task has been on the list for three weeks. It isn't hard; you've done harder things this month without noticing. But every time you sit down to start, something else suddenly needs doing first. The inbox. The dishes, even. The dread grows with the delay, which makes starting harder, which feeds the dread.

Read the restShow less

Procrastination is one of the best-mapped territories in behavioral science, and the map beats the standard advice to try harder. Present bias explains why the current moment always outbids the future one. The planning fallacy explains why the task looked small when you took it on. The Zeigarnik effect explains the background hum of the unfinished. On the tools side, implementation intentions and WOOP convert vague intentions into plans that carry their own trigger, and behavioral activation starts from action rather than waiting for motivation to show up first.
