Theme · personal & at work

Negative self-talk

The inner commentary that reviews everything you do and rarely files a positive report.

How it runs — and where to break it

Arguing with your thoughts

  1. causeA harsh automatic thought shows up
  2. fighting gives it the floor→causeYou argue with it — and lose
  3. →consequenceThe thought gets louder and stickier
  4. don't argue — defuse→toolCognitive defusion
  5. or change the tone→toolThe self-compassion break

There's a commentator. It has an opinion on the email you just sent and on the way you handled this morning. The opinions are rarely mixed reviews. Most of us run some version of this voice; what varies is the volume, and whether we've noticed it's a voice at all rather than a live report on how we're actually doing.

Read the restShow less

This part of the map holds the machinery behind that voice and the tools built to work with it. Negativity bias explains why one mistake outshouts ten things done well. The ABC model and the thought record are ways to catch what the critic said and check it against the evidence. The self-compassion break and cognitive defusion take a different route: changing your relationship to the voice instead of debating it. Cognitive behavioral therapy, the approach most of these tools come from, ties the set together.
