Theme · personal & at work

Big decisions

High-stakes choices, where anchors, losses, and sunk costs quietly do more voting than the pro-con list.

The spreadsheet has been open for a week. Pro and con columns, maybe a weighted score. And still the decision refuses to resolve, because every time you lean one way, the other side files an appeal. Big decisions are where our biases operate with real money and real years on the table, which is also why this territory is so well mapped.

Read the restShow less

Anchoring explains how the first option became the reference point. Loss aversion explains why the downside of switching looms larger than an upside of the same size. Sunk costs keep voting when they shouldn't; confirmation bias turns research into ratification; the mere exposure effect makes the familiar option feel safer purely for being familiar. The Dunning–Kruger effect is a calibration check on the confidence itself, and the fundamental attribution error covers decisions about people. The premortem is the working tool: assume the choice failed, then ask why.
