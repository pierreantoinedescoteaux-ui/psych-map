Attentional Bias

Whatever already worries us is exactly what our eyes snag on first.

Use

Useful to recognize why a current worry keeps popping up everywhere you look.

Attentional bias is the way certain things reliably grab our attention over everything else nearby, usually because they match a current worry, craving, or threat. Someone anxious about spiders scans a room and finds the one spider in it before anything else registers. Someone trying to quit smoking notices every cigarette ad, every smoker on the corner, every pack left on a table — the world seems to fill up with the very thing they're avoiding. Nothing about the environment changed. What changed is which parts of it got past the filter and which got skipped over entirely.
