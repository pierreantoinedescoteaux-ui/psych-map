Suggestibility

We often absorb someone else's suggestion as if it were our own idea or memory.

Use

Useful to notice when a leading question or someone else's framing has quietly become your own opinion or memory.

Suggestibility is how readily we accept and act on an idea, question, or instruction someone else plants, sometimes without noticing we've absorbed it. A witness asked "how fast was the car going when it smashed into the other one?" will often recall a worse crash than one asked about a car that merely "hit" the other — the word choice becomes part of the memory itself. Suggestibility isn't limited to memory: it also shows up in hypnosis, medical placebo response, and everyday persuasion, where a confidently phrased suggestion can shift what we notice, believe, or later report.
