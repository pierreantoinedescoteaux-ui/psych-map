Levels of Processing Effect

How deeply you think about something while learning it predicts how well you'll remember it later, not how many times you repeat it.

Use

Useful to know that repeating information barely helps memory; connecting it to meaning does.

The levels of processing effect is the finding that how deeply you engage with information while learning it, not how many times you repeat it, determines how well you remember it later. Skimming a word list for its letters barely leaves a trace. Asking what each word means, or how it connects to your life, builds a memory that survives. A student who rereads a chapter five times often remembers less than one who stops to explain a single paragraph out loud. The extra thinking is what makes the difference, not the extra exposure.
