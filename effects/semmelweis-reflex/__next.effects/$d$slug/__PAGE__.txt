6:T48e,<p>The goal being protected is the whole structure a person stands on: training, reputation, decades of practice, and the self-image of someone who has been helping. Accepting the new evidence meant accepting a career&#39;s worth of unwitting harm. Framed that way, the rejection wasn&#39;t stupidity — it was the belief system defending its load-bearing walls. The clash between what the evidence said and what accepting it would mean about oneself is dissonance at institutional scale.</p>
<p>A caveat the name itself deserves: this is a historical label, not a measured effect. No experiment isolates a &quot;Semmelweis reflex&quot; beyond the well-studied mechanisms it bundles — confirmation bias, belief perseverance, motivated reasoning. Its value is as a memorable worst case rather than a distinct finding.</p>
<p>The modern version rarely involves anyone dying; it sounds like an idea being dismissed in the first five seconds of a meeting because it implies the current process was wrong. The diagnostic is latency. Considered rejection takes time; the reflex is instant, and its speed is proportional to how much the evidence would cost if true.</p>
