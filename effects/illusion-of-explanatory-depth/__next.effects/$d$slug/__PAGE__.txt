2:T4d0,<p>The engine is a confusion between familiarity and mechanism. We can recognize a zipper, use one, and picture one, and that fluency feels like understanding its interlocking teeth. We also lean, invisibly, on knowledge stored elsewhere — in experts, in manuals, in the device itself working reliably — and mistake access for possession. Nothing flags the difference until an explanation is due, because holding a vague sense of &quot;gears and physics, roughly&quot; and holding the actual causal chain feel identical from inside.</p>
<p>The stakes rise when the object is a policy instead of a toilet. Philip Fernbach and colleagues found that people held confident, extreme positions on policies they could barely sketch the mechanics of — and that asking them to explain how a policy would actually produce its effects, step by step, moderated both the confidence and the extremity. Asking for reasons didn&#39;t; reasons are easy to generate. Mechanism is where the illusion fails.</p>
<p>That gives the handle: before relying on an understanding, run the Rozenblit test on yourself. Explain the thing aloud, causally, from input to output, and treat every gap you hit as the actual current state of your knowledge.</p>
