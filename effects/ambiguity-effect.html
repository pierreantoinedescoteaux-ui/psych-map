Ambiguity Effect

Given a choice, we'll take the known odds over the better bet with unknown odds.

Use

Useful to notice when you're avoiding a good option only because its odds aren't spelled out.

The ambiguity effect is our preference for a bet with known odds over one with unknown odds, even when the unknown option is objectively just as good or better. Offered an urn with 50 red and 50 black balls, or a second urn with 100 balls in an unknown red-black split, most people bet on the first urn — for red and for black. The actual odds in the second urn are unknowable, but they average out to the same fifty-fifty split. What we're avoiding isn't bad odds. It's not knowing the odds at all.
