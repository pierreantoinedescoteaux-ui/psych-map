Evidence note

This concept is TDL's own alternate name for the backfire effect; Wood & Porter 2019 ('The elusive backfire effect') found corrections generally reduce false belief across large samples, undercutting the strong reversal story the name implies.

Belief Perseverance

Once we've formed a belief, disproving the evidence behind it rarely disproves the belief.

Use

Useful to notice when a correction hasn't actually changed your mind, just your excuse.

Belief perseverance is our tendency to keep holding a belief even after the evidence that originally supported it has been shown to be false. In a well-known study, people were given fake feedback rating their judgment as either strong or weak, then told plainly that the feedback had been fabricated. Belief in their own skill level barely moved. The original reasoning had already built a small structure of supporting memories and stories, and demolishing the foundation didn't take the structure down with it. Corrections often work far less cleanly than we'd like to believe.
