14:T4ba,<p>Colin Camerer, George Loewenstein, and Martin Weber named the effect in 1989, showing that better-informed traders couldn&#39;t set their private information aside even when ignoring it would have made them money. Susan Birch and Paul Bloom later found the same leak in a purer form: adults who knew where an object had been moved couldn&#39;t cleanly reason about a person who didn&#39;t, especially when the truth felt plausible.</p>
<p>You&#39;ve been on both ends. The senior engineer explains the deploy process in four sentences, each resting on three things the new hire hasn&#39;t met yet, and reads the polite nodding as understanding. Every textbook written by a master, every onboarding doc, every set of driving directions from a local carries the same signature — steps that were once hard-won have become invisible, so they go unsaid.</p>
<p>The curse doesn&#39;t yield to trying harder, because the missing experience is precisely the one we can&#39;t simulate. The workable handles are external: test explanations on someone who genuinely doesn&#39;t know, watch where they stall, and treat every &quot;obviously&quot; in your own draft as a flag for a step you&#39;ve stopped seeing.</p>
