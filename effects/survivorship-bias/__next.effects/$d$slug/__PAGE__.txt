2:T445,<p>The classic illustration comes from the Second World War. The statistician Abraham Wald was asked where returning bombers should carry extra armor, and the bullet holes clustered on the wings and fuselage. Wald&#39;s answer ran the other way: those planes had survived being hit there. The armor belonged where the returning planes were clean, because hits in those spots were the ones that kept planes from coming back to be counted.</p>
<p>The same filter runs quietly through ordinary life. A founder&#39;s memoir explains the habits behind the win, but the thousands who kept the same habits and folded never wrote books. A fund family advertises the strong ten-year record of its surviving funds after the weak ones were closed and merged away. A gym&#39;s wall of testimonials is, by construction, written entirely by people who stayed.</p>
<p>The practical handle is to ask what the full sample would show: who started down this path and isn&#39;t here to report back, and what took them out. When the failures are invisible, the visible cases prove much less than they seem to.</p>
