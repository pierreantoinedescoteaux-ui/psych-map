14:T473,<p>George Loewenstein, who developed the idea, showed its teeth in medicine: healthy people confidently predict what they&#39;d want as patients, and clinicians who aren&#39;t in pain systematically underrate the pain of those who are. Leaf Van Boven and Loewenstein added the social version — people who had just exercised judged lost hikers to be more bothered by thirst than by hunger, projecting their own state onto strangers in a different situation entirely.</p>
<p>The domestic version is a Tuesday-night classic. At 2pm, full from lunch, you plan a light dinner and one episode before bed. At 9pm the plan meets the actual evening self, who orders the pizza and watches four. Neither self is lying. Each one simply can&#39;t simulate the other from where it stands.</p>
<p>The handle follows from the mechanism: stop asking the cold self to forecast, and let it legislate instead. Decide the exit time before the party, the spending cap before the store, the response before the difficult conversation — and when judging someone mid-hot-state, remember you&#39;re reading their situation through your current temperature.</p>
