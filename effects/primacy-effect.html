The Primacy Effect

We remember the first items in a list better than the ones buried in the middle.

Use

Useful to put the most important point first if you want it to stick.

The primacy effect is our tendency to remember the first items in a sequence better than the ones in the middle. Read someone a grocery list of fifteen items and, days later, they'll recall the first few — milk, eggs, bread — long after the middle stretch has dissolved into a blur. The lead items get more attention and more rehearsal in the moment, which is what lets them make the trip from short-term into lasting memory. It's one half of the serial position effect; the tail end of a list gets its own separate advantage, for a different reason.
