Hyperbolic Discounting

A reward today feels disproportionately more valuable than the same reward next week, even if the wait barely matters.

Use

Useful to explain why you keep picking a smaller reward now over a bigger one later, and to build in commitments that remove the near-term choice.

Hyperbolic discounting is our tendency to value a reward far more when it's available right now than when it's delayed, in a way that doesn't scale evenly with time. Offered $50 today or $60 next week, many people take the $50. Offered $50 in a year or $60 in a year and a week, the same people wait for the $60 — same one-week gap, opposite choice. The value of "later" isn't discounted at a steady rate; it drops off a cliff the moment something becomes immediate. That steep near-term pull is a big part of why long-term plans lose to short-term temptation.
