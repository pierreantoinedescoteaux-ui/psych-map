2:T47e,<p>The goal being protected is self-worth. An honest ledger of causes would charge some failures to our own account, and that entry hurts, so the attribution system routes it elsewhere. Miller and Ross opened the modern debate in 1975 by asking whether the pattern even needed a motivational story; a 2004 meta-analysis by Mezulis and colleagues settled the size question, finding one of the largest average effects in social psychology, present in nearly every population sampled — though notably smaller in East Asian samples and in people with depression.</p>
<p>The everyday version runs both directions at once. A quarter with good numbers confirms the new strategy; a quarter with bad numbers gets attributed to the market. Same dashboard, two bookkeeping systems. The cost isn&#39;t vanity — it&#39;s that miscredited causes make results unrepeatable. If the win was partly luck, next quarter&#39;s plan overestimates control; if the loss was partly us, the fixable part goes unfixed.</p>
<p>A workable check is to write the explanation you&#39;d give if the outcome had gone the other way, and see which causes survive both versions.</p>
