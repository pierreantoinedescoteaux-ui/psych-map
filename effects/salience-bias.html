Salience Bias

We give more weight to whatever's loudest, brightest, or most emotional in the room, even when it's not the most important thing there.

Use

Useful to notice when a flashy detail is driving your decision more than the facts that actually matter.

Salience bias is our tendency to give more weight to information that stands out — because it's vivid, unusual, loud, or emotionally charged — while quieter, more representative information gets discounted. What grabs attention gets treated as what matters most, whether or not that's true.

Read the restShow less

Reading twenty reviews of a restaurant, nineteen calm and positive, you fixate on the one describing a health inspector's visit. That single vivid detail colors the whole decision more than the other nineteen combined, not because it's more informative, but because it's the one that's hard to stop picturing.
