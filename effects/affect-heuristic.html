Affect Heuristic

We ask how we feel about something and treat the answer as an assessment of the facts.

Use

Useful to separate a gut reaction from an actual risk-benefit analysis before deciding.

The affect heuristic is our habit of using a quick feeling — liking or disliking something — as a stand-in for actually weighing its risks and benefits. If a technology feels good, we rate it as both safer and more useful than a neutral read of the evidence would support; if it feels bad, we downgrade both at once, even though real risk and real benefit are separate questions. A nuclear plant that sounds ominous gets rated more dangerous and less worthwhile in the same breath, by the same gut check, without the two judgments ever being checked against each other.
