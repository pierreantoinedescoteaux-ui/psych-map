Evidence: mixed — show the evidence note

Evidence note

Basic semantic and perceptual priming replicates reliably, but the famous social/behavioral priming demonstrations are contested — Doyen et al. (2012) failed to replicate Bargh et al.'s (1996) elderly-priming/walking-speed effect except under experimenter-expectancy conditions.

Priming

Seeing one thing quietly changes how we respond to the next thing.

Use

Useful to notice when something you read or saw moments ago is steering your next reaction.

Priming is what happens when exposure to one thing quietly shapes how we respond to the next, without our noticing the connection. Read the word "yellow" and you'll recognize "banana" faster than if you'd just read "car" — the first word activates a web of related ideas that the second word can then ride in on. Basic priming like this, word to word or image to image, is one of the most reliable findings in cognitive psychology. The flashier claim — that priming a stereotype can change someone's walking speed or grip strength — has fared much worse under closer testing.
