Evidence note

List (2003) found the endowment effect shrinks or disappears among people experienced in a given market, indicating its size depends on trading experience rather than being universal.

Endowment Effect

Owning something makes us value it more than we would if it were still for sale.

Use

Useful to notice when you're pricing something based on the fact that it's yours, not on what it's actually worth to a buyer.

The endowment effect is the gap between what we'd pay for something and what we'd charge to give it up, once it's already ours. Hand someone a coffee mug at random and ask what they'd sell it for, then ask a second group with no mug what they'd pay for the same one — the owners consistently ask for more than the non-owners offer. Nothing about the mug changed. Only who was holding it did. The effect shows up in negotiations, decluttering, and pricing decisions, wherever ownership itself — rather than the item's actual market value — quietly sets the number in someone's head.
