Identifiable Victim Effect

We'll go out of our way to help one named person in front of us, but feel far less pull toward a large, faceless group.

Use

Useful to notice when a single vivid story is moving you more than statistics that represent far more people in the same situation.

The identifiable-victim effect is our tendency to feel more urgency to help one specific, named person than a much larger group described only in numbers. A fundraising appeal built around one child's photo and story routinely outperforms an appeal citing thousands of children in the same need, even though the larger number represents far more suffering. A face and a name give the mind something concrete to hold onto; a statistic stays abstract, no matter how large it is. The result is that generosity often tracks how vivid a victim feels, not how many people actually need help.
