The Representativeness Heuristic

We judge how likely something is by how much it resembles our mental picture of a typical case, not by the actual odds.

Use

Useful to catch yourself sizing up a probability by 'does this fit the stereotype' instead of checking the real numbers.

The representativeness heuristic is our habit of guessing how likely something is by asking how much it resembles a mental template, instead of checking how common it actually is. A case that "feels like" the typical example gets rated as probable, even when the real numbers say otherwise.

Read the restShow less

Told that Sarah is quiet, loves organizing spreadsheets, and reads for fun, you guess she's more likely to be a librarian than a nurse — even though nurses vastly outnumber librarians and plenty of them read quietly too. The description matched your picture of "librarian." The population odds never entered the calculation.
