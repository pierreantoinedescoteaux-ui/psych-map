Naive Allocation

Given several options, we tend to split our resources evenly across all of them instead of weighing which ones are better.

Use

Useful to catch yourself dividing money, time, or attention equally by default instead of by what each option deserves.

Naive allocation is our tendency to split a limited resource evenly across the options in front of us, regardless of whether those options actually deserve equal weight. Offered three retirement funds, most people put roughly a third into each rather than working out which funds fit their goals — and if a fourth fund is added, the split becomes roughly a quarter each, not a recalculated best mix. Even split feels safe and fair. It also means the number of options on the table, not their quality, ends up deciding where your money goes.
