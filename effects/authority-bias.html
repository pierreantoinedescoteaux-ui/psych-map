Authority Bias

A claim feels truer the moment it comes from someone in a white coat or a suit.

Use

Useful to double-check advice on its merits, not on the title of whoever gave it.

Authority bias is our tendency to give someone's claim more weight simply because of their title, uniform, or credentials, rather than the quality of the claim itself. In one classic study, hospital staff followed a phone instruction from an unfamiliar 'doctor' to administer a clearly excessive drug dose, at a rate far higher than surveys predicted they would. Nobody checked the dosage against the chart. Nobody verified the caller. The label 'doctor' did the convincing on its own. The same shortcut runs quietly through ordinary life: a claim in a lab coat travels further than the same claim in street clothes.
