Distinction Bias

Two options look far more different side by side than they do once you're actually living with one.

Use

Useful to remember that comparing options head-to-head can make small differences feel decisive when they won't matter later.

Distinction bias is what happens when two options are placed next to each other: the gap between them looks bigger than it will ever feel in ordinary use. Comparing two apartments floor plan to floor plan, the extra 40 square feet seems significant. Living in either one, a week in, the difference barely registers. The side-by-side view manufactures a level of contrast that daily experience doesn't sustain, because daily life rarely puts the alternative in the next room for comparison. The bias makes side-by-side comparison shopping feel more consequential than it usually turns out to be.
