Long-standing decision-theory finding (Loomes & Sugden's regret theory tradition); no named replication controversy.

Regret Aversion

We pass up a good option to avoid picking the one that might leave us kicking ourselves.

Use

Useful to catch yourself choosing the conventional option just to avoid the sting of a personally-caused bad outcome.

Regret aversion is our tendency to shape a decision around dodging a future bad feeling, not around getting the best outcome. Before choosing, we quietly run the tape forward: how would it feel to have picked this and been wrong? The uncomfortable answer nudges us toward whatever choice would sting least if it backfired.

Read the restShow less

You're picking a mutual fund. The index fund performs about the same as the one your friend recommends, but if the recommended one drops, you'll have only yourself to blame. So you pick the index fund — not because the numbers favor it, but because losing on your own choice would hurt more than losing on the safe one.
