False Consensus Effect

We assume more people agree with us than actually do.

Use

Useful to double-check an opinion you're sure is 'obviously' shared before treating it as consensus.

The false consensus effect is our habit of guessing that our own opinions are shared by more people than they actually are. Ask someone who skips breakfast to estimate what share of people also skip it, and the number comes in high — higher than real survey data supports. The same pattern shows up with political views, parenting choices, and taste in music: whatever a person believes, they tend to treat as the more typical position. It's an easy slip to make, since your own reasoning is the one example you have from the inside, while everyone else's is a guess made from the outside.
