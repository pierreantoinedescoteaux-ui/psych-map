6:T455,<p>The original 1998 experiment: hungry participants who resisted fresh cookies and ate radishes instead gave up on an unsolvable puzzle in half the time of participants who got the cookies. Resisting seemed to spend something. Hundreds of studies followed, and the idea spread into productivity books and courtroom arguments.</p>
<p>It didn&#39;t survive scrutiny. The supporting literature shows the statistical pattern of publication bias, where small positive studies get published and null results stay in drawers. When 23 labs ran a preregistered version with over 2,000 participants (Hagger et al., 2016), the effect was indistinguishable from zero.</p>
<p>One thing survives. Mental fatigue over long stretches without breaks is real, and it needs no fuel tank to explain it. The best-known rescue attempt has not fared as well: Job, Dweck and Walton reported that the depletion pattern appeared mainly in people who believe willpower is limited, but when 36 labs tested the paradigm with 3,531 participants, they found neither the depletion effect nor the mindset moderation (Vohs et al., 2021).</p>
