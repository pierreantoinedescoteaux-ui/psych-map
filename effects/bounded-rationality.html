Bounded Rationality

We don't optimize decisions — we settle for the first option that's good enough given the time and information we actually have.

Use

Useful to explain why a 'good enough' choice under real constraints isn't sloppy thinking — it's the sane response to limited time and information.

Bounded rationality names the fact that nobody actually weighs every option before deciding — there isn't enough time, information, or mental bandwidth for that. Instead of hunting for the best possible choice, we stop at the first one that clears a 'good enough' bar, a process economist Herbert Simon called satisficing. Renting the third apartment you toured because it has decent light and a fair price isn't a failure of rigor; it's what a mind built to work within real limits actually does. The theory reframes plenty of so-called irrational choices as sensible shortcuts given the constraints.
