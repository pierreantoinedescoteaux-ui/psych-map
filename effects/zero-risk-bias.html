Zero Risk Bias

We'll pay more to bring a risk all the way to zero than to cut a bigger risk by the same amount.

Use

Useful to catch when 'completely eliminated' is swaying a decision more than the size of the risk reduction warrants.

Zero-risk bias is our pull toward eliminating a risk entirely rather than reducing a bigger risk by the same amount. Offered a choice between cutting a rare contamination risk from 1% to 0%, or cutting a much more common risk from 10% to 5%, many people pick the first option, even though the second saves more overall. Getting to zero feels categorically different from getting to "smaller," because zero removes the need to keep worrying about it at all. That comfort is worth paying for, but it isn't the same thing as getting the safest outcome available.
