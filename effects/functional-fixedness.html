Functional Fixedness

We see an object only by its usual job, which blocks us from noticing its other uses.

Use

Useful to remember when you're stuck on a problem that a tool you already have might solve, once you stop seeing it as only one thing.

Functional fixedness is our tendency to see an object only in terms of its usual job, which makes it hard to notice its other possible uses. A hammer reads as "the thing you hit nails with," full stop — not as a paperweight, a lever, or a doorstop. Duncker's classic candle problem showed the pattern cleanly: given a candle, a box of tacks, and a match, most people struggle to realize the tack box itself can become a candle holder, because in the setup it appeared to be a mere container. Once an object's conventional use is fixed in mind, alternative uses go dark, even when they'd solve the exact problem in front of you.
