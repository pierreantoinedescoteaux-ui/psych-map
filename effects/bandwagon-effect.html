The Bandwagon Effect

The more people already believe something, the truer it starts to feel to us too.

Use

Useful to separate what you actually think from what's just popular right now.

The bandwagon effect is our tendency to adopt a belief or behavior simply because a lot of other people already have. A restaurant with a line out the door gets more customers than an empty one serving the same food, because the line itself becomes the recommendation. Voters shift toward whichever candidate is reported to be leading, not because the polls changed their views on policy, but because leading looks like being right. Popularity substitutes for evidence, and once enough people are on board, being on board starts to feel like its own kind of proof.
