Hindsight bias

We remember outcomes as more predictable than they were — once we know the ending, we feel we saw it coming.

Use

Useful to judge past decisions by what was knowable at the time, instead of scoring them against an ending nobody had yet.

Hindsight bias is our tendency, once we know how something turned out, to feel the outcome was predictable all along — and often to misremember having predicted it. Knowing the ending quietly rewrites the middle. The uncertainty we actually lived through gets harder and harder to reinstate.

Read the restShow less

Baruch Fischhoff demonstrated the mechanism in 1975: people given the outcome of a historical episode rated that outcome as more foreseeable than people who didn't have it, and then underestimated how much the answer had helped them. Neal Roese and Kathleen Vohs later mapped the bias into three stacking layers — misremembering what we predicted, believing the event was inevitable, and believing we personally foresaw it.

You can watch it work after any breakup, layoff, or lost match. The signs were all there, we say — but the signs were assembled afterward, by a memory that now knows which details count as signs. Before the ending arrived, the same details sat in a pile with dozens of others pointing every direction.

The cost is that we grade decisions by their outcomes rather than by the information available when they were made, which makes good process look stupid when it loses and reckless process look brilliant when it wins. The workable counter is a paper trail: write predictions down before outcomes arrive, and audit old decisions against what was on the table at the time.

  • flip side ofeffect

    Planning fallacy

    The same flattening runs both ways in time: looking back, the mess settles into an obvious story, and looking forward, the mess has not shown up yet, so the plan looks clean.

  • leads toeffect

    Optimism bias

    Remembering that we saw past outcomes coming makes our record as forecasters look better than it was, and the next prediction gets made with confidence that record never earned.
