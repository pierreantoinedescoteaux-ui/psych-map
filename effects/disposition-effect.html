Disposition Effect

We sell our winning investments too fast and hang on to losers too long.

Use

Useful to catch yourself avoiding a loss on paper by refusing to sell, even when the money would do more elsewhere.

The disposition effect describes a lopsided habit investors have: cash out the stocks that went up, keep the ones that went down. Selling a winner feels like banking a victory. Selling a loser feels like admitting the purchase was a mistake, so it stays in the account, unsold, waiting to get back to even. The stock doesn't know its purchase price — only the investor does, and that number keeps shaping decisions long after it should matter. The pattern shows up across retail trading accounts and tends to lower overall returns, since it works exactly backward from a simple rule: let winners run, cut losers.
