2:T4b0,<p>Daniel Read and Barbara van Leeuwen ran the clean demonstration in 1998: office workers chose a snack to receive the following week. Asked right after lunch, most chose fruit. Asked in the late afternoon, hungry, most chose the junk food — for a moment seven days away, when today&#39;s hunger would be long gone. George Loewenstein, Ted O&#39;Donoghue, and Matthew Rabin formalized the bias in 2003 and traced its fingerprints through real markets, from catalog orders of winter gear driven by the weather on ordering day to durable purchases made at emotional peaks.</p>
<p>The pattern is easy to spot once named. Grocery lists written hungry feed a different person than the one who unpacks them. January&#39;s motivated self signs a year-long gym contract for eleven months of a self it won&#39;t remain. The newly heartbroken furnish an entire imagined future out of one bad month.</p>
<p>The handle is timing and delay. Notice which state you&#39;re forecasting from, and for any commitment that outlives the mood — contracts, purchases, resignations, tattoos — either decide in a neutral state or impose a waiting period long enough for the current one to pass through you first.</p>
