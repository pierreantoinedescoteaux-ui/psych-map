Impact Bias

We picture a future win or loss hitting us harder, and for longer, than it actually will.

Use

Useful to discount how big and lasting you expect a future event's emotional impact to be.

The impact bias describes how badly we misjudge the emotional weather of our own future — both how strong it will be and how long it will last. Picture someone convinced that landing a particular job will make them happy for years. They get the job, feel the spike, and within a couple of months are back to arguing about whose turn it is to do the dishes, same as before. The mind spotlights the single event and forgets that ordinary life — the traffic, the errands, the next thing to worry about — keeps arriving and diluting it.
