Decoy Effect

Adding a worse third option changes which of the first two we pick.

Use

Useful to spot when a deliberately bad option on a menu or price list is there to steer your choice, not to be bought.

The decoy effect shows up when adding a third, clearly worse option changes what we choose between the original two — even though the decoy itself is never bought. A cinema sells small popcorn for $3, large for $7, and medium for $6.50. Almost nobody wants medium. But next to it, large stops looking greedy and starts looking efficient, so more people buy large than would have without medium on the board. The decoy does no direct work; it just makes one of the real options look better by comparison. Menus, subscription tiers, and salary bands use this on purpose.
