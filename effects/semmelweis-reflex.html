Evidence note

A named historical pattern, not a lab-tested effect — the evidence base belongs to its ingredients (confirmation bias, belief perseverance, motivated reasoning), not to the reflex as its own phenomenon.

Semmelweis reflex

We reject out of hand any evidence that would overturn what our work, training, or worldview is built on.

Use

Useful to hear your own instant 'that can't be right' as a signal to slow down — the speed of the rejection is the tell.

The Semmelweis reflex is the reflexive rejection of evidence that contradicts an established belief system — not weighing and dismissing, but refusing at the door. The name honors a specific casualty. In 1847 Ignaz Semmelweis showed that when doctors in his Vienna maternity clinic washed their hands in chlorinated lime, deaths from childbed fever collapsed. The medical establishment largely rejected the finding; germ theory didn't exist yet, and the claim implied that physicians' own hands had been killing their patients. Semmelweis died in an asylum in 1865, his protocol unadopted.

The goal being protected is the whole structure a person stands on: training, reputation, decades of practice, and the self-image of someone who has been helping. Accepting the new evidence meant accepting a career's worth of unwitting harm. Framed that way, the rejection wasn't stupidity — it was the belief system defending its load-bearing walls. The clash between what the evidence said and what accepting it would mean about oneself is dissonance at institutional scale.

A caveat the name itself deserves: this is a historical label, not a measured effect. No experiment isolates a "Semmelweis reflex" beyond the well-studied mechanisms it bundles — confirmation bias, belief perseverance, motivated reasoning. Its value is as a memorable worst case rather than a distinct finding.

The modern version rarely involves anyone dying; it sounds like an idea being dismissed in the first five seconds of a meeting because it implies the current process was wrong. The diagnostic is latency. Considered rejection takes time; the reflex is instant, and its speed is proportional to how much the evidence would cost if true.
