14:T48f,<p>The mechanism runs through where we look for evidence. We assess other people by their behavior, but we assess ourselves by introspection — and bias leaves no fingerprint in conscious experience. Motivated reasoning feels exactly like reasoning. So the inward check comes back clean every time, and the cleanliness gets read as objectivity rather than as the limit of the instrument. Later work by Irene Scopelliti and colleagues found the blind spot is largely unrelated to intelligence; sharper thinkers are not safer.</p>
<p>You can watch it in any hiring debrief. Everyone in the room can name the others&#39; soft spots — one interviewer overweights pedigree, another favors candidates who remind him of himself. Ask each person about their own ratings and the answer is some version of &quot;I just went with the strongest candidate.&quot;</p>
<p>The practical move is to stop consulting the feeling. Whatever rate of bias we can see in our peers is a decent estimate of our own, since we are running the same machinery, and the correction lives in process — stated criteria before the fact, comparisons made blind — not in resolving to be fair.</p>
