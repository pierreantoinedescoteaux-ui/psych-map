2:T597,<p>Dan Kahan and colleagues named the pattern in 2007, and its most uncomfortable claim concerns ability. Across Kahan&#39;s studies, greater numeracy and scientific literacy did not converge people toward the evidence on contested questions; the most capable reasoners were often the most polarized, on the reading that skill at analysis is also skill at defending a position. That claim is the part of the picture standing on the thinnest ground — see the caveat below. This is motivated reasoning with a social engine — the goal protected isn&#39;t comfort or ego but membership.</p>
<p>There&#39;s an unsettling rationality underneath. One person&#39;s view on climate policy changes the climate not at all, but visibly changes how their community receives them. Given those stakes, protecting the identity is the locally sensible move; the damage appears only when everyone&#39;s locally sensible moves aggregate into a public that can&#39;t agree on facts. Worth noting: how much of this is truly <em>motivated</em> is contested — people with different priors can look biased while updating honestly — and the headline motivated-numeracy experiment did not survive a preregistered replication with about 3,000 participants.</p>
<p>A usable tell is imagining your position reversed and asking who you&#39;d have to argue with at dinner. If the answer stings, the question has stopped being purely factual for you.</p>
