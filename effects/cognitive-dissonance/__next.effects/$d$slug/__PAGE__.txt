2:T423,<p>Leon Festinger proposed the theory in 1957, and the 1959 experiment with James Carlsmith gave it its signature result. People paid $20 to tell the next participant a boring task was fun stayed honest with themselves — the money explained the lie. People paid $1 had no such cover, and they resolved the tension by deciding the task really had been sort of interesting. The smaller the external justification, the bigger the internal rewrite.</p>
<p>You commit to eating better, then order the burger. Within minutes a case assembles itself: long week, protein matters, starting Monday is cleaner anyway. The goal being protected is a consistent self — someone whose actions make sense — and the rewrite is fast, quiet, and sincere. Run often enough, it doesn&#39;t just excuse single choices; it slowly relocates the belief until the standard matches the behavior.</p>
<p>The tension itself is the useful part. Felt early, it&#39;s information that a choice and a value have diverged — which of the two gets edited is still open at that point.</p>
