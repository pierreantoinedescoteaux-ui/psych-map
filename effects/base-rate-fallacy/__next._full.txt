17:T467,<p>Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky traced the mechanism in 1973: we predict by resemblance. If the description resembles our image of a librarian, the base rates barely move the estimate, no matter how lopsided they are. Maya Bar-Hillel&#39;s later work showed the pattern holding across problem after problem — people don&#39;t lack the statistics, they rank them as less relevant than the individuating story.</p>
<p>The expensive version involves tests. Say a screening test catches a rare condition 95 percent of the time, and one person in a thousand has the condition. A positive result feels like 95 percent certainty. Run the numbers and most positives come from the huge pool of healthy people triggering the occasional false alarm — the true chance can sit under 2 percent. The same arithmetic ambushes hiring signals, fraud flags, and security alerts.</p>
<p>The handle is a fixed order of operations: establish how common the thing is before looking at the details of the case, then ask how much the details should really move that starting number. Specifics adjust a base rate; they don&#39;t replace it.</p>
