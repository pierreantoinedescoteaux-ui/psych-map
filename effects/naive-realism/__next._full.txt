17:T415,<p>The trouble starts before any argument does. Perception hides its own work: by the time an event reaches awareness it has already been filtered, weighted, and captioned, and it arrives labeled as fact. There is no inner signal distinguishing &quot;the world&quot; from &quot;my processing of the world,&quot; so a difference in interpretation gets experienced as the other person defying reality.</p>
<p>At dinner, a relative makes a remark about your job. You heard a jab; your partner heard an awkward attempt at interest. Neither of you experiences an interpretation — you both experience what happened, and the disagreement afterward is not about the remark anymore. It is about why the other person refuses to admit the obvious.</p>
<p>The workable handle is to treat disagreement as information about differing inputs before treating it as information about the other person. &quot;What are you seeing that I&#39;m not?&quot; is a better opening move than a diagnosis, because on this evidence the diagnosis is usually premature.</p>
