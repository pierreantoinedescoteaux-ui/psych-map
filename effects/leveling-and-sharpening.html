Leveling and Sharpening

Retelling a story quietly smooths out most details and blows up a few, until the sharpened bits are all that's left.

Use

Useful to notice that a story retold many times keeps only its sharpest points, not its most accurate ones.

Leveling and sharpening describes what happens to a memory each time we retell it: most details flatten out, a few get exaggerated, and the story that survives is a caricature of what actually happened. Tell a friend about a chaotic dinner party, and the first version is full of names and side notes. By the fifth telling, the guest list has shrunk to 'everyone,' the food is a footnote, and the one plate that broke has become the whole story. Nothing was faked. Memory just keeps whatever makes the story hang together, and lets the rest go quiet.
