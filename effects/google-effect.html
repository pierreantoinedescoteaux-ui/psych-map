Google Effect

We forget information more easily once we know it's just a search away.

Use

Useful to notice why facts you could look up slip your mind faster than facts you can't, and to plan what's actually worth memorizing.

The Google effect, sometimes called digital amnesia, describes how we're less likely to hold onto information once we know it's easy to find again online. Ask a friend a trivia question and watch what happens when they realize they could just check their phone — the motivation to actually retain the answer drops. It's not that search engines make us worse at remembering in general; it's that memory is selective about what's worth the effort, and anything permanently retrievable gets deprioritized. The same logic explains why people often recall where to find information more reliably than the information itself.
