14:T42b,<p>Michael Norton, Daniel Mochon, and Dan Ariely pinned it down in 2012. People who assembled a plain storage box were willing to pay noticeably more for it than people bidding on an identical pre-built one, and amateur origami folders priced their lopsided cranes near what expert work would fetch — while expecting other people to agree. Completion turned out to be the trigger: when builders had to dismantle their creation, or never finished it, the premium evaporated.</p>
<p>The everyday cost isn&#39;t overpriced furniture. It&#39;s the manager who can&#39;t kill the process she designed, the developer defending the tool he wrote over the better off-the-shelf one, the homeowner listing the self-renovated kitchen above what any buyer will pay. Labor bought love, and love is now doing the pricing.</p>
<p>The handle is to separate the two questions the effect merges: how good is this, and how much of me is in it? Getting a valuation from someone who didn&#39;t build the thing answers the first question without the second one leaning on the scale.</p>
