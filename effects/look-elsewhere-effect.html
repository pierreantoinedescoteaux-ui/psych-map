Evidence note

Well-documented statistical/methodological pattern in significance testing; no named controversy over its existence.

Look-elsewhere Effect

Keep testing a hunch enough times and pure chance will eventually hand you something that looks significant.

Use

Useful to catch when a 'significant' finding is really the product of enough retries, not a real effect.

The look-elsewhere effect names what happens when a search for a meaningful result is allowed to keep going after the first attempt comes up empty. Test enough variables, enough subgroups, enough re-runs, and one of them will cross the threshold for 'significant' by chance alone — the same way flipping a hundred coins in a row will eventually produce ten heads in a row somewhere. The finding feels earned because it took effort to find. It was actually guaranteed to appear, given enough looking, whether or not anything real was there.
