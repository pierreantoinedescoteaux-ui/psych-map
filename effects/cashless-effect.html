Long-standing finding on payment-method spending premiums, no named replication controversy.

The Cashless Effect

Paying with a card feels lighter than paying with cash, so we spend more when the money never becomes physical.

Use

Useful to notice why a tap-to-pay purchase feels easier to say yes to than counting out the same amount in bills.

The cashless effect describes how paying without physical money makes spending feel easier and less painful, which makes us more willing to go through with a purchase. Handing over bills involves a small, visible loss — watching the stack get thinner — that a tap or swipe simply doesn't reproduce. Someone who would hesitate over a sixty-dollar cash purchase taps a card for the same amount without a second thought, because the card converts a loss into an abstraction. The less a payment feels like parting with something, the less resistance it meets on the way out.
