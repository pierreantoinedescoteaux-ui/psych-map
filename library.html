  • Behavioral activationmodelSchedule the activity first and let the mood follow, because in a slump motivation is the last thing to come back.
  • Cognitive defusionmodelNoticing a thought as a sentence your mind produced, not a fact to believe or an order to follow.
  • Graded exposuremodelA ladder from the mild version of a dreaded thing to the real one, climbed one boring rung at a time.
  • Implementation intentionsmodelAn if-then sentence that decides in advance when, where, and how you'll act, so the moment doesn't get to renegotiate.
  • The ABC modelmodelA three-step frame for catching the belief that sits between an event and how you end up feeling.
  • The habit loopmodelCue, routine, reward: the three-part circuit that runs your repeated behavior, and the handles for changing it.
  • The premortemmodelBefore the plan starts, assume it has already failed and have everyone write down why.
  • The self-compassion breakmodelA one-minute reset for painful moments: name the sting, remember it's human, and speak to yourself like a friend.
  • The thought recordmodelA worksheet that puts a hot thought on trial: evidence for, evidence against, and a fairer verdict.
  • WOOPmodelWish, Outcome, Obstacle, Plan: imagine the goal achieved, then face the thing in you that keeps stopping it.
  • Acceptance and commitment therapyapproachThe therapy that stopped arguing with thoughts and started changing our relationship to them.
  • BehaviorismapproachThe school that said psychology should study what people do, not what they report feeling.
  • Cognitive behavioral therapyapproachThe family of approaches that treats thoughts as behavior you can notice, test, and change.
  • PsychoanalysisapproachThe original talking cure, built on the claim that symptoms mean something — and that the meaning is hidden from the person who has them.
  • Rational emotive behavior therapyapproachThe first cognitive therapy, built on the claim that events don't disturb us — our beliefs about them do, and beliefs can be disputed.
  • Big decisionsthemeHigh-stakes choices, where anchors, losses, and sunk costs quietly do more voting than the pro-con list.
  • BurnoutthemeEffort keeps going out, nothing registers coming back, and rest has stopped working.
  • Following throughthemeThe gap between the plan you made and the person who was supposed to run it.
  • Negative self-talkthemeThe inner commentary that reviews everything you do and rarely files a positive report.
  • ProcrastinationthemeKnowing exactly what needs doing, wanting it done, and watching yourself do anything else instead.
  • Worry and ruminationthemeThe mental loop that replays the past and rehearses the future without ever reaching a verdict.
