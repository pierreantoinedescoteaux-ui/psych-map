Goal · personal

Worry less

Let worries pass through as thoughts instead of convening the full committee.

The 2 a.m. planning sessions get shorter. A worry arrives, gets noticed as a thought rather than obeyed as an instruction, and passes without the two-hour committee meeting. The theme this points to covers why the loop is so convincing and the tools that work on your relationship with worry instead of trying to win against it.

    Availability heuristic

    Vivid, easily imagined disasters feel more probable than they are — most of what a 'what if' spiral runs on.

  • →Featureseffect

    Impact Bias

    The feared outcome hits less hard and lasts less long than you picture, which undercuts the premise worry runs on.

  • →Featureseffect

    Pessimism bias

    Overestimating how likely bad things are is close to a plain description of a worry-prone default setting.

  • →Featureseffect

    Illusion of control

    Worrying feels like doing something useful about an outcome you can't actually steer.

  • →Featureseffect

    Regret Aversion

    A lot of 'what if' worry is pre-emptive regret management rather than risk assessment.
