17:T43e,<p>CBT began as a failed replication. In the early 1960s Aaron Beck, then a psychoanalyst, set out to verify the analytic account of depression as hostility turned inward. His data showed something else: depressed patients ran streams of spontaneous negative thoughts — about themselves, the world, and the future — that were biased in consistent, describable ways. Beck published the cognitive model in 1967 and spent the next decade building cognitive therapy around it, borrowing behavior therapy&#39;s discipline of measurement and homework while making thought itself the target.</p>
<p>He was not the first to make the cognitive turn — Albert Ellis had been disputing beliefs since 1955 — but Beck&#39;s stream arrived with manuals and trial data, and the two converged into the CBT family through the 1970s and 80s. Today CBT has the largest evidence base of any psychotherapy, with strong trial support for depression and the anxiety disorders. The debate that remains is comparative: whether it outperforms other structured, bona fide therapies is still contested.</p>
