13:T43f,<p>Psychoanalysis began with a patient. In <em>Studies on Hysteria</em> (1895), Josef Breuer and Sigmund Freud reported that hysterical symptoms faded when patients traced them, under treatment, to memories they could not otherwise reach — one patient named it &quot;the talking cure.&quot; Freud pushed the idea further than Breuer would follow: the blocked material was not just forgotten but <em>repressed</em>, pushed out of awareness because it was unacceptable. Against the neurological consensus of the 1890s, which read hysteria as a disorder of the nervous system, Freud made it a disorder of meaning.</p>
<p>Nearly every later school of therapy either descends from psychoanalysis or defined itself against it, and its vocabulary — denial, projection, defense mechanisms — became everyday language. Its scientific standing is another matter. The core claims resist the controlled testing later approaches were designed around, and by modern trial standards the evidence base is thin, though briefer psychodynamic therapies have accumulated respectable trial support.</p>
