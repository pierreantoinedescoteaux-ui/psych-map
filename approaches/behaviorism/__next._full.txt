13:T418,<p>Behaviorism was declared, not discovered. In 1913 John B. Watson published &quot;Psychology as the Behaviorist Views It,&quot; arguing that psychology should be a natural science whose goal is the prediction and control of behavior. His target was the introspectionist method that then dominated the field — trained observers describing their own conscious experience, in the tradition of Wundt and Titchener — which Watson considered unverifiable. Pavlov&#39;s conditioning work supplied a mechanism; B. F. Skinner later extended the program with operant conditioning, showing how consequences sculpt behavior without any appeal to what the organism thinks.</p>
<p>The strict version did not survive the cognitive turn. By the 1960s it was clear that language, memory, and expectation do real explanatory work, and psychology re-admitted the mind. But the learning principles held up — conditioning effects replicate about as well as anything in the field — and behavior therapy never left. It survives as the &quot;B&quot; in CBT.</p>
