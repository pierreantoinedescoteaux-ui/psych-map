Think better

Thinking fails in four recognisable ways: the brain cuts corners, fails to see its own errors, bends toward what it wants to be true, or simply lacks the inputs. The first three get better the moment you can see them coming — that’s what this field is for.

Why we misjudge our own abilities

We get the conclusion, never the reasoning that produced it.

  • Dunning-Kruger effect
  • Imposter syndrome
  • Bias blind spot
  • Hindsight bias
  • Illusion of control
  • Illusion of explanatory depth
  • Choice blindness
  • Introspection illusion

Why we believe what we want to believe

Some beliefs hold up who we are, so we defend them.

  • Confirmation bias
  • Cognitive dissonance
  • Motivated reasoning
  • Self-serving bias
  • Identity-protective cognition
  • Semmelweis reflex

Why we break promises to our future self

We simulate tomorrow using how we feel right now.

  • Optimism bias
  • Planning fallacy
  • Present bias
  • Hedonic adaptation
  • Projection bias
  • Empathy gap
  • End-of-history illusion

Why easy examples and first numbers win

Speed of recall stands in for how common something is.

  • Anchoring
  • Availability heuristic
  • Base rate fallacy
  • Mere exposure effect
  • Peak–end rule
  • Survivorship bias
  • Zeigarnik effect

Why we judge others by different rules

We know our reasons; with others we only see behaviour.

  • Fundamental attribution error
  • Naïve realism
  • Solomon's paradox

Why bad moments hit harder than good ones

Missing one threat cost more than missing many gains.

  • Loss aversion
  • Negativity bias
  • Ostrich effect

Why we can't let go of what we started

What we work on becomes part of us, so it stays.

  • Sunk cost fallacy
  • IKEA effect
  • Moral licensing

Why we assume others see what we see

Our own mind is the only sample of minds we have.

  • Curse of knowledge
  • Spotlight effect
