Think better

Thinking fails in recognisable ways: we cut corners, we miss our own errors from the inside, we bend toward what suits us. Seeing one coming is what this map is for.

Too much information

More arrives every second than a mind can hold, so nearly all of it is filtered out before it is ever noticed.

Need to act fast

Waiting for certainty costs more than being wrong, so the call gets made on what is already in hand.

Not enough meaning

The fragments that get through rarely add up, so the gaps between them are filled with a story.

Outside the four

Benson's map doesn't cover these — mostly what happens when a mind turns its attention on itself.

What to remember

Nothing can be stored whole, so an experience is edited on the way into memory.

Deciding and judging

Weighing options, sizing risks, calling which way to go — the part of thinking that has to commit.

Everything else

Mostly the mind's read on itself: what it knows, what it will feel later, how much of it shows.

Reading other people

Working out why someone did what they did, from the outside, with none of their reasons in view.

Reading the odds

Rates, chances and samples — the arithmetic end of thinking, run on intuition instead.

Remembering

What gets stored, and what the storing quietly changes about it.

Why we judge others by different rules

Why bad moments hit harder than good ones

Why we assume others see what we see

  • Curse of knowledge
  • Spotlight effect
