A letter · why this exists

I read a lot of psychology to pass the time. Not for a degree, not for work. I find it interesting, and I keep wanting to go one level deeper than whatever article I just finished.

That kept not working. The good writing is out there, but every route into it assumes I already know the name of what I’m looking for. If I walk in knowing only that I keep putting things off, there is nowhere to start. So I built the thing I wanted to use, because building software is the part I’m actually trained for.

It’s a map. You can look at all of it at once, group it three different ways, or filter it down to the concepts behind a situation you recognise. Every entry says where it came from.

A few decisions worth explaining.

Contested findings are marked contested. Dunning-Kruger largely reproduces with random data. Ego depletion didn’t replicate. A psychology resource that repeats those as settled isn’t worth reading, and the badge costs nothing.

Concepts are only linked where there’s a stated reason for the link. It would have been easy to generate a related-concepts list for every entry and end up with a graph that looks impressive and means nothing. An empty space is more honest than a made-up edge.

There’s no AI in the product. I could have put a model behind the search box. Keyword matching is instant, free, and doesn’t fail during a demo, and I’d rather show that I know when not to reach for one.

It isn’t finished. Two of the three sections are still being built, and I’d rather say that on the page than pretend otherwise.

I built it for myself and then put it online, on the assumption that I'm probably not the only one who didn't know where to start.

— P-A
